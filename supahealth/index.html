The problem

I built this app to help me more easily manage my body fat percentage. I tried several calories tracking apps initially (MyFitnessPal, CalAI), but despite hitting the daily target I still hadn't seen any progress after ~6 weeks. I realized this was because my targets were off and I didn't have an accurate count of calories I was burning on a given day (which varied, while goals were static). I decided to learn about body composition management (ie. safe/optimal targets) to understand appropriate surplus/deficit, and then build an app that: 1 - gave you targets based on your biometrics, and 2 - provided a responsive calories target that flexed based on your activity level for the day.

The solution

In v1, I worked with Chatgpt to build a very simple monolith webapp version (everything lived in Supabase). This was a cool POC, but overall pretty janky, so I recruited my SWE friend help develop the foundation for a real app: a FastAPI + Postgres backend. After he gave me the basic program, I spent two weeks working with Cursor to understand the entire flow from client json to executing CRUD commands. Once I was familiar, I started developing the business logic and Swift frontend.

To get an accurate calorie count, the backend ingested data from several real-time data streams (iPhone, Apple Watch, Withings Scale), which was paired with physical activity level (PAL) multipliers.

On the nutrition side, information was seeded from external datasets such as USDA's public food database, and Open Food Facts (for barcode scanning). For support, I developed a chat feature which queried & seeded long term data (ie. goals/progress) and short term data (weekly fitness/nutrition) to give the LLM appropriate context for any questions the user may ask. Eventually I had the frontend live on Testflight, the backend on AWS and 15 users beta testing.

Agents

Once I began beta testing, I configured several agents to speed up product development, backed by PostHog analytics:

  • Database seeding agents: Two agents, each with access to Tavily. One focused on seeding foods from restaurants, the other CPG products. I gave each agent access to a list in Linear, and each night they would research companies on their respective lists and populate the global database.
  • Analytics agent: One agent, focusing on reviewing PostHog analytics and suggesting improvements in user flow or potential bugs. This agent would create Linear tickets based on what it found.
  • User activity agent: This agent would review PostHog analytics specifically for user engagement metrics, helping to identify opportunities for improving the user onboarding/first 7-day experience.
  • Automation agent: This agents purpose was to review actions taken by me over the past several days, and identify repetitive tasks I was completing that could be automated.
  • Founder agent: This agents purpose was to review actions taken by me over various periods of time, and give me objective feedback on whether I was moving the needle or focusing on low-value tasks.
