The problem

In college, I had a few friends who had instagram 'foodie' accounts where they would review restaurants. Whenever I needed to make a dinner reservation, I would always skim through these Instagram accounts and pick something from one of them. It was a great solution, but Instagram isn't designed for restaurant discovery.

With the only real alternatives (at the time) being Yelp and Google Review, which to this day is still often just boomers complaining, I thought it would be cool to build a restaurant discovery platform based around how Gen Z search and share restaurant recommendations. We (my cofounder and I) were pitching to our friends 'if TikTok and Yelp had a baby.'

The incubator

We worked on Flyerz as a team within San Diego State University's ZIP Launchpad Incubator. This incubator helped guide us to build a viable company, with a product that people actually want, rather than building something nobody wants or would pay for. This guidance was provided through several high caliber resources:

  • Program structure: To move forwards in the program we had to prove the market was unsatisfied with the current solutions, and that there was a genuine need for our value proposition.
  • Customer discovery: We did 50 consumer interviews and ~15 restaurant interviews before designing a survey to get more unbiased feedback.
  • Domain experts: I met with a startup lawyer for entity selection (C-corp vs LLC) and IP risk, a software expert to review our technical plan, a UX expert to review the prototype.
  • Legal: We developed a Founders agreement, NDAs, and intern agreements.
  • Interns: We recruited our designer and developer, and attended workshops on hiring and general management.
  • Advisory board: We presented our research to the incubator's advisory board each semester for strategic guidance and continuance in the program.
  • Seed funding: We were awarded several rounds of seed funding to cover early stage expenses.

Takeaways

Ultimately we decided to shut down Flyerz due to lack of a viable business model, however, I had many takeaways from trying to start my first company. The top 2 were:

  1. Build something people (you don't know) actually want. We were building something a few of our friends wanted, and actively avoided gathering honest market sentiment. The incubator called bullshit on this - as they should have - and helped us understand how to pursue true product market fit.
  2. Spend time deeply understanding the potential business models. From the start, we knew the two obvious business models (advertising and business subscription) were going to be an uphill battle. A deeper assessment of this on day one could have helped us realize the potential dead ends we were pursuing.
